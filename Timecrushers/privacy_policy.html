Privacy Policy

TimeCrusher — Hobby Studio

Effective date: November 21, 2026.

This policy explains how Hobby Studio handles information when you use the TimeCrusher browser game hub, its multiplayer game server, and this policy page. The production frontend is available at timecrushers.pages.dev. Game chips and points are virtual game values, not cash or redeemable prizes.

You do not need to create an email/password account or sign in with Google to play. Choose a display name that does not reveal sensitive personal information.

Information We Process

  • Player profile: Your chosen display name, avatar or initials, a generated profile tag, and a browser-session player identifier are used to identify you in a room.
  • Multiplayer sessions: The server processes room codes, game settings, participants, moves, cards, scores, connection status, and other game state to run matches and restore interrupted connections.
  • Text chat: Messages you send, your player identifier, display name, and message timestamps are relayed to other connected participants in the room.
  • Optional voice chat: If you enable your microphone and grant browser permission, audio is sent through the game server to other room participants. You can switch the microphone off at any time. TimeCrusher does not request camera access or precise device location.
  • Connection and diagnostic information: Hosting providers process network and request information, such as IP addresses and request times. The game server logs operational information including room codes, player identifiers, display names, game actions, dealt cards, and message payloads. These payloads can include chat text and encoded voice data.
  • Support correspondence: If you email Hobby Studio, we receive your email address, message, and any attachments you choose to send.

Browser Storage and Cached Assets

TimeCrusher uses local storage to remember your display name, profile tag, selected avatar, and theme. Session storage keeps your player identifier, room code, game type, variant, and joined name so that a page reload can reconnect to the same game. Your browser may restore session storage when restoring tabs.

A service worker caches local artwork and app icons. It excludes the game homepage, scripts, stylesheets, API calls, and cross-origin backend requests from its caching. You can remove these preferences, session details, and cached assets using your browser's site-data controls for the TimeCrusher address you used.

Clearing browser data does not erase information already delivered to other players, support correspondence, or hosting logs. Preferences stored on one website address do not automatically transfer to another address.

How We Use Information

  • Operate multiplayer rooms, apply game rules, distribute game updates, and reconnect players.
  • Display player identities and deliver room text and optional voice chat.
  • Remember browser preferences and deliver static game assets efficiently.
  • Diagnose errors, investigate abuse, protect the service, and respond to support or privacy requests.

The current TimeCrusher frontend does not include Google AdSense, third-party behavioral analytics, or social sign-in. Reading this policy or using the game is not blanket consent to future advertising or other processing that requires separate consent.

Information Visible to Other People

Your display name, avatar, player identifier, and public game activity may be visible to room participants and spectators. Text and voice chat are shared with connected room participants. Public-room discovery exposes room codes, game types, variants, and participant counts; the homepage shows an aggregate player count.

A private room is not a private messaging service: someone with its code may be able to join or spectate. Do not share passwords, payment details, personal addresses, or other sensitive information in your display name, chat, or voice conversation. HTTPS/WSS protects connections in transit, but chat and voice are processed by the server and are not end-to-end encrypted.

Hosting and Third-Party Services

Cloudflare Pages hosts the production frontend, and Microsoft Azure hosts the multiplayer server and its operational logging. This Hobby Studio policy page is hosted on GitHub Pages and loads fonts from Google Fonts. Those providers receive connection information when their services are used. The TimeCrusher frontend itself uses local fonts and artwork.

Advertising and Consent

Advertising is not currently enabled in TimeCrusher. If we introduce Google AdSense, we will update this policy and configure the necessary notices and consent controls before activating ads.

Where Google ads are enabled, Google and other advertising providers may use cookies, local storage, or similar technologies to serve and measure ads. Depending on your choices and applicable requirements, these technologies may support personalized ads based on visits to this and other websites. Learn how Google uses information from partner sites at Google's partner-site information page and manage Google ad personalization at My Ad Center.

When personalized ads are offered to visitors in the EEA, UK, or Switzerland, we will use a Google-certified consent management platform. Any legally required consent for advertising storage or data processing will be requested separately, with a way to revisit your choices. No advertising-consent controls are active while ads are disabled.

Retention and Security

Game rooms and their state currently reside in server memory. Disconnected, inactive rooms are eligible for scheduled cleanup after approximately ten minutes without activity, and server restarts clear in-memory state. These events do not automatically delete operational logs. The browser keeps up to 200 received chat messages in memory during a session.

Browser preferences remain until you clear them. Hosting logs and support correspondence have separate retention arrangements; no fixed deletion period is promised here. Contact Hobby Studio for information about applicable retention or a deletion request. Some records may need to remain for security investigations or legal obligations, and provider backups or logs may have additional limits.

The production site uses HTTPS/WSS connections. No online service can guarantee absolute security. Turning off the microphone, leaving a room, or closing the game stops the corresponding live transmission, but does not undo information already shared or logged.

Your Choices and Privacy Requests

You can change your display name and avatar, switch off the microphone, leave a room, and clear browser site data. Where applicable law gives you rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, or object to processing, contact Hobby Studio so we can assess and respond to your request. You may also have the right to complain to your local data-protection authority.

If you believe a child has shared personal information without appropriate permission, contact us. Avoid sending additional sensitive information while making a request.

For support or privacy questions, use the Contact Us section on the Hobby Studio homepage or email hobbystudio25@gmail.com. Include the game name and enough detail to identify the issue, but never send passwords or authentication tokens.

Changes to This Policy

We will update this page and its effective date when our practices change. Material changes, including introducing advertising, may require additional notices or consent. Please review the policy periodically.
